Slovakia beyond Bratislava_

  • Bratislava sits in the far west corner. Most of the country is east and north of it, in the hills.
  • Trnava and Trenčín are easy by train. For Štiavnica, Bojnice and Orava a car helps a lot.
  • Orava is far enough that I'd stay a night.

Trip details from slovakation.com and travelslovakia.sk, both run by Slovak guides (and both sell tours). Orava also from slovakia.travel.

    1 · Trnava

           +        +        +        +
      /^\      /^\      /^\      /^\       "little rome"
     |   |    |   |    |   |    |   |
   __|___|____|___|____|___|____|___|__
    • Half an hour by train. Easiest trip on this page.
    • People call it "Little Rome" because of all the churches.
    • Go up the town tower (1574) for the view over the roofs.
    • Large parts of the medieval town walls are still standing.
    • Small and calm. A good half day, and it has its own Christmas market in December.

    2 · Trenčín + Beckov

                     _|_
            _   |   |   _
           | |__|   |__| |
           |  [] [] []   |       trenčín castle
   ________|_____________|___________
  /   rock with a roman inscription   \_____
    • About 1 h 15 on a direct train. The castle is right above the main square.
    • One of the biggest castles in the country. Climb Matúš tower for the view.
    • On the rock under the castle there's a Roman inscription from 179 AD, left by legionaries who wintered here. It's about as far north as the Romans got in this part of Europe.
    • Beckov is a ruin on a cliff above a village, 30 to 40 minutes by bus from Trenčín. Worth adding if you have a car.
    • In winter there's a small ice rink in the centre.

    3 · Banská Štiavnica

             _[]_                    _[]_
      __|    |__   ___   ___   |    |___
     |  old castle| | | | | |  new castle|
  ___|________|___|_|_|_|_|_|__|____|____|___
     \   houses down the sides of a crater   /
      \_____________________________________/
    • Old silver-mining town on UNESCO's list, built in the hills inside an old volcanic crater.
    • About 2 hours by car. By public transport it's 3 to 4 hours (train to Žarnovica or Zvolen, then bus), so stay over if you can.
    • Old Castle, New Castle, the mining museum (you can go underground), the Kammerhof.
    • Lots of small cafés and guesthouses. It gets busy at weekends.
    • Around Christmas there's a large carved nativity scene in the Holy Trinity church.

    4 · Bojnice + Čičmany

              /\    /\         /\
         /  \  /  \   /\  /  \
        |    ||    | /  \|    |     bojnice: the fairy-tale one
        | [] || [] ||    | [] |
     ___|____||____||____|____|___
    • The castle everyone has seen in photos. Rebuilt about a hundred years ago to look like a French château.
    • Inside are period rooms with the original owner's furniture and art. It belongs to the Slovak National Museum.
    • The town also has a zoo, a spa and local craft beer.
    • From Bratislava: train to Prievidza, then a local bus, about 3 hours. By car about 2.
    • Čičmany is a village of wooden houses painted with white patterns. People still live in them. Without a car it's hard to reach, so only combine it with Bojnice if you're driving.

    • Castle stacked up a cliff about 100 m above the river. The most striking one in Slovakia, and it was used for the old film Nosferatu.
    • It's in the far north, roughly 3 hours by car. Make it a weekend, or stop on the way to the Tatras.
    • Combine with the wooden churches in Leštiny or Tvrdošín (UNESCO), or the open-air village museum in Zuberec.
    • In winter there's skiing in Zuberec and on Kubínska hoľa. Check castle opening times before you go in the cold months.

    6 · In winter

    • Bratislava and Trnava both have Christmas markets until just before Christmas.
    • Banská Štiavnica is lovely in snow and has the nativity scene.
    • Orava and the mountains are for skiing and snowshoeing. Roads can be slow.
    • Some castles cut their hours or close in winter. Check first.
