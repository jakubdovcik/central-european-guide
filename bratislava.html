Bratislava_

  • Small capital on the Danube. Vienna is an hour away, Budapest two.
  • The old town takes 15 minutes to cross on foot. Walk it slowly anyway.
  • Late November to Christmas the main squares turn into Christmas markets.
  • Currency is the euro.

    This is the part I'd spend most time on. You don't need a plan. Go in at Michael's Gate, get lost in the side streets, and stop for coffee twice.

    Christmas markets now

    • Main Square and Hviezdoslav Square, from late November until just before Christmas.
    • Get lokše (thin potato pancakes), punch or mulled wine, and walk between the stalls.
    • Evenings are busiest and best.

    Michael's Gate & the lanes map

    • The one gate left from the medieval walls. You can go up the tower.
    • Under the arch there's a "kilometre zero" plaque with distances to other capitals.
    • Michalská and Ventúrska run south from here, full of cafés and bars.

    Main Square & Primate's Palace map

    • Old Town Hall with a tower you can climb, and the Roland fountain from 1572.
    • The pink palace behind it has a Hall of Mirrors where the Peace of Pressburg was signed in 1805.
    • Upstairs: English tapestries from the 1600s, found hidden in the walls during repairs.

    St. Martin's Cathedral map

    • Hungarian kings and queens were crowned here from 1563 to 1830, Maria Theresa too.
    • There's a gilded crown on top of the spire. Small brass crowns in the pavement mark the old coronation route.
    • A 1970s highway runs right past it. Locals still complain.

    Čumil map

    • The bronze man looking out of a manhole on Panská street.
    • Honestly, a slightly awkward one. It's a tourist photo stop and not much else, and locals don't make a fuss about it.
    • If you pass by, fine. Don't go out of your way.

    Hviezdoslav Square

    • Long promenade with trees, ending at the old National Theatre (1886).
    • Opera and ballet tickets cost a fraction of what Vienna charges.

    Bratislava Castle map

    • The white block with four towers on the hill. Burned in 1811, rebuilt in the 1950s and 60s.
    • The grounds and the baroque garden are free. From the walls you see the river, the bridges and Petržalka.
    • Go up before sunset and walk back down the old steps into town.

    Slavín map

    • Soviet war memorial on the hill above the castle, about 6,800 soldiers buried there.
    • Quiet, few tourists, and the widest view of the city.
    • 20 to 30 minutes uphill through a neighbourhood of villas and embassies.

    UFO bridge · skip it

    • The saucer on top of Most SNP. It looks good from below.
    • I wouldn't pay to go up. The restaurant is fine, nothing special, and the castle and Slavín have better views for free.

    Bratislava has a lot of good cafés, and the coffee is better than you'd expect. Vienna is famous for its old coffeehouses. Bratislava is more about small places that roast their own beans or buy from good roasters, and the baristas know what they're doing.

    • KOFI Karadžičova. map
    • Strettko & Granita old town: coffee and brunch. map

    Blue Church map

    • St. Elizabeth's, Art Nouveau, by Ödön Lechner (1907–13). Pale blue all over, roof tiles included.
    • The inside is usually only open around mass. 10 minutes east of the old town.

    The river and the old park map

    • Walk the embankment downstream to Eurovea.
    • Or cross Starý most (trams and people only) to Sad Janka Kráľa, a park from the 1770s. It's often called the oldest public park in Central Europe.

    Socialist-era buildings

    • Slovak Radio, the upside-down pyramid. map
    • Kamenné námestie with Hotel Kyjev and the old Prior department store.
    • Slovak National Gallery: a 1970s wing on legs over an old barracks. The permanent collection is free. map
    • Petržalka, the big housing estate across the river. Easiest to take in from the castle.

    Half-day trips. For Trenčín, Orava and other places further out, see [s]lovakia.

    Devín Castle map

    • Ruin on a rock where the Morava flows into the Danube. Austria is on the other bank.
    • Below it, the Gate of Freedom remembers people shot trying to cross the Iron Curtain here.
    • Bus 29 from under the UFO bridge, about 25 minutes.

    Danubiana map

    • Modern art museum at the tip of a strip of land in the Danube, 15 km south.
    • Easiest by car or bike along the dam.

    Wine villages map

    • Rača is still inside the city (take the tram). Svätý Jur, Pezinok and Modra are 20 to 40 minutes away.
    • Small family cellars. Ask for Frankovka, Veltlínske zelené or Riesling.
    • Modra also makes the blue-and-white folk pottery.
    • Open-cellar weekends around St. Martin's Day (November) and in spring.

    Červený Kameň map

    • Castle in the Small Carpathians, about an hour by direct bus (stop "Častá, Červený Kameň").
    • Inside it's a museum of how the nobility lived. Pair it with a cellar in Modra on the way back.

    6 · One day

        09:00 ----- 11:00 ----- 13:00 ----- 15:00 ----- 17:00 ----- 20:00
      |           |           |           |           |           |

    1. Coffee at oproti or Kauka.
    2. Michael's Gate, then wander: Main Square, Primate's Palace, St. Martin's.
    3. Lunch: fried cheese at Lokal Unas, or crêpes at Palacinka Lacinka.
    4. Up to the castle, then on to Slavín for sunset.
    5. Back down to the Christmas markets, then Nuda or Mirror Bar.

    Getting around

    • From Vienna airport there's a direct bus, about 50 minutes. From Bratislava airport, bus 61.
    • Trains to Vienna every hour, about an hour. In summer there's also a boat.
    • Trams and buses: IDS BK app, or tap a bank card on board.
    • Taxis: order through Bolt, Uber or Hopin. Don't take one off the street.
